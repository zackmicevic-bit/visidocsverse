◈ GLOSSARY OF TERMS
/to focus/ — the keyboard shortcut for the search field: pressing / anywhere outside a text field jumps the cursor straight into Search.
active body — the document currently in focus. It is shown in the Active-body reader pane and gilded gold on the canvas. There is at most one per canvas; clicking empty space releases it back to the general view. It never moves for being selected — the pick is marked where it sits (gold halo, bold label, hot tethers, gold inventory rows); the sun itself (the gravitational center) is crowned through an inventory dot box (theme/tag rows carry dots; document rows carry an eye-preview plus a + #tag writer instead), which moves it home.
active project+ — the project canvas (tab) currently on screen, named in the top bar. Each project keeps its own documents, formation, sun, and lens; + starts a new empty one.
associate project — linking verses together: right-click any tab for its associations menu and tick another verse (links are always mutual). Associated projects share an accent color, and uploads, copies, deletes, and twin lockstep span the whole group.
associated documents○ Labels — the grounded member documents behind a theme orb in the Thematic view. Right-click the orb for "Show all associated documents" (with or without name labels): every member appears as a small paper icon ringed around the orb — the top grounding documents in gold when that theme is selected, in blue otherwise, the rest in a lighter shade of it. Files no theme grounds are still shown, palest of all, each riding the single orb whose terms hit its text hardest (or the largest orb when nothing hits) — so every file in the project displays. Icon count is per-theme attachments, not a file census: a grounded file can ground in up to two themes and then appears on both orbs, so the ringed icons can outnumber the project's files (e.g. 17 icons for 15 files when two files are shared). Left-click an icon to light up that document's row in the reader's bodies inventory (grounded bodies plus the ungrounded files riding that orb, listed in their own section below); right-click it to remove that document from this canvas. Right-click empty canvas to show every theme's documents at once, or to clear them all away. The icons never impinge on neighboring orbs: they shrink and spill into wider rings as membership grows. The bottom pill's Labels button (next to Hover peek) flips the name labels on every associated document currently in view — orb labels are untouched and stay on the left-panel Labels row; with nothing in view it explains how to show documents first. The overlay is session-only — switching canvases or leaving the Thematic view lifts it.
Autosaved (project) — every change is stored automatically inside the app (the top bar stamps the time: "autosaved …"), and projects reopen on their own next launch. This is separate from "save project", which downloads a backup file to your computer.
backlinks — the documents on this canvas that reference a file by its title. Every general-view row reports them (“N backlink(s)”), and title-links are also what pull a body into something's orbit.
body▼ — one file, drawn as one orb (or one ▼ triangle with Body to ▼ on). A body carries its file's words, mass, tags, and note; click one to make it the sun.
carousel⤢↻‹ — the Active body re-read as a curved left/right-scrollable coverflow of glass panes floating over the canvas (two-pages button under the reader's stretch arrow). The reader's text is cloned into at least 3 pages (center pane flat, sides swung back), with the body's pictures riding the last page in their original format. Arrows, wheel, drag and ←/→ keys all page; any click outside the panes, ✕ or Esc closes it. Double-click any pane (or its ⤢ button — or either jump-to-original icon: the reader-edge button or the one beside the "Double click to zoom" caption) to open the whole original document over the carousel — reader format with its pictures, its own zoom slider (100–200%, Ctrl+scroll works too), scrolled to that page; Esc returns to the carousel. Photo, video and audio originals play on the first page; extracted text and tagged pictures follow, spreadsheets preview as a table on the last page, original one click away.
center of gravity — whatever the universe orbits around. With a focused document it is the sun; with no active body (general view) the canvas center itself pulls with sun-strength gravity so the formation holds together.
copy all content — duplicate a whole project tab's documents into another tab: right-click the source tab and choose ⧉ Copy all content, then right-click the destination tab and choose ⤓ Paste N bodies from "X". It works across universes — no association needed. Each pasted body arrives with its text, tags, pictures, notes, sticky-spot anchors and original file, and stays linked to its origin; a copied body never resurrects a name the destination deleted or merged away. Bodies already present under the same name are skipped, so pasting twice reports everything is already here. The copied stash survives reloads and stays ready for repeated pastes into several tabs.
delete tag — erase a tag from the whole canvas: open ALL TAGS, hover the pill and click its ×, then confirm — every body's #tag is stripped from its text and auto-derived copies of that name are suppressed so title words can't grow it back. Explicitly writing #tag in any body brings it back. Deletes affect the current canvas only. Tick “Don't ask again” to skip future confirmations; the Confirms row in the tags pane tips turns them back on.
disassociate project — unlinking a verse: untick its last link and it goes isolated (◌ dashed grey tab with its own identity color). Uploads land only in that tab, copies neither leave nor enter, deletes span only it, and its formation evolves independently.
document icons ("Body to" button) — the bottom-pill "Body to" button (page icon) beside "Orb to ▼": shape swap — every document and tag body renders as a plain white page icon with a folded corner, ruled with three text lines (gold for the focused body), the same screen size as the orb it replaces. Photo textures, gradients and glow stay off while labels, tethers, sticky-note squares and selection rings stay exactly as they are; while Rotation spins, pages flip edge-on like cards (the same card-flip as triangles); theme bodies keep their orbs. Only available in MAIN and TAGS view modes. Shape-exclusive with triangles — turning one on turns the other off. Toggling off restores the orbs.
document viewsLocate document in — MAIN, THEMATIC and TAG as seen from one document's side: the "Locate document in :" box beside the reader shows where the shown document sits in each view. MAIN lights the document's own orb on the Main canvas; THEMATIC enters the Thematic view on the theme that grounds it; TAGS enters the Tags view with every tag it carries lit gold together. Each jump also centers the canvas on its target (the whole tag group for TAGS). The reader keeps the document throughout — only the canvas moves. Not to be confused with the whole-universe views (Main (Universe) view, thematic view, tags view, switched from the Universe view modes row): those apply the same three lenses on the macro level, recomposing the entire canvas — all documents, themes and tags at once — while document views apply them on the micro level, to a single document. More generally, any document shown in the reader keeps its orb lit gold on every canvas where it has a body — parked or focused, selection or not.
explicit tag#tag+ #tag — a #hashtag typed directly into a document’s text: a plain green pill with no badge. Its purpose is grouping — it declares “these documents belong together” in your own words: bodies sharing it pull together with a tether, share weight, light up jointly in search, and appear as one node in the Tags view (e.g. #thesis_core on three bodies groups those three). It lives in the prose itself, so editing the words can add or remove it — unlike a manual tag, which is pinned to one passage from the menu (green pill + gold M). Both are yours and always count (blue auto tags only repeat title words and are ignored when too common); writing #name in any body (re)creates the tag everywhere, including resurrecting a deleted one. Use the + #tag button in the reader's tag row to write one in-app (appended to the body's text).
Export handoff⤓ Export research handoff (.txt)… — a one-file research brief for AI chats: Open Project → ⤓ Export research handoff (.txt)… writes every note you wrote, grouped per document, each followed by the workspace documents most similar to it, with the shared words shown so every link is auditable. Copies of a document share one notepad, so each lineage appears only once. Nothing is uploaded anywhere — the file is built and saved on this machine; paste it into any AI chat (it opens with a suggested drafting instruction).
files (adding)⤓ Folder⤓ Files — your files stay right where they are. Adding a file or folder (left-pane ⤓ Folder / ⤓ Files) never moves, copies, or alters the originals on your hard drive — the app only reads them.

Instead it builds a lightweight visual duplicate (a "rendered clone") inside the canvas — extracted text plus image previews — so you can map, explore, and connect your documents without touching your original work.
general viewSee Tags Inventory — the canvas with no active body: the center holds gravity and the reader lists the LARGEST BODIES (plus View all bodies), each with word count, mass, and backlinks. The See Tags Inventory button, which opens ALL TAGS, lives in the top pill.
Main (Universe) viewMAIN — the regular canvas universe: one body per document, orbiting the focused sun (or the canvas center with nothing active). This is the default view and the one both lenses rebuild — leaving the Thematic or Tags view always returns here with the formation untouched.

The Analogy: Think of your library as a Solar System, where every document is an individual planet floating in space.

How It Works: In your daily work, when you click on a file, it acts like a gravitational anchor—the Sun—drawing all related files into its orbit so you can see what directly connects to it. When nothing is selected, your files rest in their default positions. Switching to other views and back is like taking a space walk: when you return, your desktop universe remains completely untouched.
mass — a file's gravitational pull: bigger, more comprehensive files weigh more and tug harder on everything around them. Shown in the meta line and Properties; a newly focused sun temporarily borrows mass 600 for stability while its real vault mass is kept safe and restored afterwards.
merge tags — fold one tag into another: in ALL TAGS, drag a pill and drop it onto another pill, then confirm — every body carrying the dragged tag is rewritten to the receiving tag, the dragged name disappears, and its auto-derived form is suppressed. Simple hierarchy: the receiving tag is the primary and keeps its name; the moved tag does not retain its name. Merges affect the current canvas only. Tick “Don't ask again” to skip future confirmations; the Confirms row in the tags pane tips turns them back on.
notesNotes — every document in your library includes a built-in shared notepad attached directly to the file itself, ensuring your notes persist. You can read and write notes from three convenient locations: a canvas icon beside the document label (which turns solid when notes exist), a quick pop-out button inside the document reader, or a dedicated glass Notes Pane on the left edge. Because notes are tied to the document itself, any entry added or edited in one view updates instantly across all open tabs.

The Notes Pane provides a complete, timestamped history along with smart time-savers like Auto-save as you type and an Add Selection feature that appends highlighted passage text directly to your notes. Highlighted text creates an interactive link — clicking the note inside the pane jumps the reader straight to that exact passage in the text, while deleting the note removes the highlight mark. Note controls appear automatically whenever a document is actively open in the reader and remain hidden during broad system or thematic overviews where no single file is selected.
Open ProjectOpen Saved Project — the left-pane button for bringing computer files back in: load a master file (replaces every open project) or merge one into the current canvas. It never touches files on your computer beyond the one you pick — day-to-day safety copies come from the save-project backup.
orbital mechanics — the physics layer: sun gravity, peer springs between linked bodies, shell tethers by link depth, and gentle braking — no blanket drag, so genuine orbits survive. New bodies and suns start at circular-orbit speed instead of teleporting; linkless comets drift outward toward the rim cap.
pills (top and bottom) — the two glass bars: the top pill (project tabs, Universe view modes, stats), worn as a book tab with a gold glowing edge (flat top, rounded bottom corners, mirroring the bottom pill's Tips tab), and the bottom pill (canvas controls + tips). Each keeps its own natural size and never stretches to match the other. The top pill's right edge never passes the bottom pill's right edge, so the pair always stays vertically aligned on the right: docked, the top pill wraps to the bottom pill's width on their shared left edge; hand-moved, it slides back left instead. The top pill never shrinks below its seated width (with zero tabs the ghost cluster itself goes live (red frame) holding a Create a project tab plus add tab placeholders across the full row instead of collapsing; with projects, the live cluster fills past the last tab with the same static add tab frames so the whole cluster stays put). No cluster frame ever shrinks — deleting a tab backfills its space with a placeholder, and every cluster shares one fixed frame width. The cluster sits centered in the pill width inside a static frame that never stretches sideways — it only grows downward by wrapping. Drag either by any blank background to move it (the position persists; the right edge always stays inside the screen); double-click the background to send it back to the dock beside the left pane.
rename tag✎ Rename tag — give a tag a new name everywhere on the canvas: right-click any pill (the Active body reader, the tag page, or the ALL TAGS card) or right-click a tag body on the canvas and choose Rename, then type the new name — every body's explicit #old is rewritten to #new, manual anchors move with it, and auto-only bodies are grounded under the new name so nothing is lost. The old name is suppressed (title words can't grow it back) and the new name is unsuppressed so it can live. Renaming onto an existing name folds in like a merge. Renames affect the current canvas only and share the delete/merge confirmation setting (“Don't ask again”; the Confirms row in the tags pane tips turns it back on).
Search bar (left pane) — Searches across documents — it finds which file(s) in the project/verse match, so you can quickly jump to a different doc. Type to spotlight every matching body while the rest fade, with a jump-to list underneath; tag pills feed it a #tag, and clearing the box — or one click on empty canvas — restores the full view.
shared ground — files holding the same (or very similar) conversation. Distinctive themes are compared across files; files sharing them are tethered together, and the reader's Shared Ground section lists them for the focused document: who it connects to, and through which shared words. The reverse direction is grounding: in the Thematic and Tag views, a theme or tag body is grounded in its member documents — the files its idea or label stands on, listed in its GROUNDED section. Shared ground fans outward from one document to its neighbors; grounded documents fan inward from one theme or tag to the files behind it.
snap to center — the canvas recentering that follows a reader pick: choosing a document from the reader to find it on the canvas eases the pan when its body already shows on-screen, and snaps fast when it sits off the edge. Right-click empty canvas in any view mode for "Snap to center: ON/OFF" — off stops every automatic recenter (rows still light gold, orbs stay where they are).
sun — the document in focus, sitting at the center and pulling everything else around it. Click any body to make it the sun; click empty space to release it — the same click also clears any tag highlight, restoring the full non-faded view.
+#tag slot frame — the +#tag button beside each document's eye in the inventory is a status frame for that body's tags, and it repaints itself whenever they change: dashed gold means the body carries tags you made yourself; solid means its tags came from the system (auto top-ups or anchors pinned onto reader passages); dashed grey means untagged — an empty slot waiting for its first tag. Press the button to assign that body a tag.
tag#tagauto/name — a #hashtag grouping: hand-written in a file, or auto-derived from its title, pictures' text, and top terms (blue auto/ pills). Matching is prefix-blind (#evidence is the same tag as auto/evidence), and tags shared across files tether bodies together and weave the tags view. A tag typed into the prose is an explicit tag; one applied from the menu is a manual tag.
Tags (manually created)#tagM — tags you make by selecting a block of text in the Active body reader, right-clicking it, and choosing Tag: the selection becomes a tag on that body, badged with a gold M on its pills. Manual tags tether, weigh, and search exactly like written #tags, but they live outside the body text — editing or deleting the words won't remove them. Remove one from the Tags pane × or by deleting its tag in the tags view. Shortcut: drag any pill from ALL TAGS onto highlighted reader text to pin that tag onto the exact passage.
tags viewTAGS — the canvas redrawn around the labels on its documents: one body per tag, webbed wherever two tags share member documents, sized by grounded-body count. Click a tag to see its grounded member documents, click a member document to open it. When a document is shown in the reader, each tag it sits in glows gold; orange lines between tags only mean shared member documents, not membership. Mutually exclusive with the Thematic view.

The Analogy: Think of a whiteboard organized by colored sticky notes, where every tag is a central pinboard.

How It Works: Instead of looking at the files themselves or deep underlying concepts, the canvas groups everything by the specific labels you or the system have attached (like #Draft, #Invoice, or #Urgent).

--Larger tag bodies mean more files are pinned under that label. -- Lines connecting two tags show overlap—documents that carry both sticky notes at the same time.

Clicking a tag opens up the folder of every document carrying that label, giving you a quick, organized view of your explicitly marked work.
the art of tagging — Tags are the labels on your documents. Three kinds live here: #explicit tags you type into body text, manual tags you pin onto a selection through the Tag menu (gold M — click one to jump back to the exact passage), and #auto tags the app derives from titles.

In your research workflow, you tag documents to create clusters of related information, which helps visualize conceptual relationships and navigate your document universe. You apply specific, logical tags—such as #Theme or #Phase—to group files effectively. These tags act as metadata, allowing the system to identify connections, reveal overlaps, and maintain structure across your research materials. Using consistent naming conventions alongside these tags improves searchability, ensuring your workspace remains organized and impactful. Ultimately, this approach transforms scattered documents into an interactive, structured workspace that makes synthesizing evidence across different sources straightforward.

To make the "art of tagging" feel intuitive, here are three easy-to-understand analogies:

1. The Supermarket Aisle vs. The Magic Scanner Traditional Folders: Imagine a physical grocery store. An apple must sit in the Produce section under Fruit. If you want to put it in "Snacks," "Baking Supplies," or "Low-Calorie Foods," you can't—unless you buy three identical apples and put one in each section.

Tagging: Imagine every item in the store has a set of invisible digital tags (#Fruit, #Snacks, #Baking, #Under100Calories). When you scan your shopping list for #Baking, the store instantly brings the apple, flour, and cinnamon together in front of you.

The Art: Tagging allows one item to exist in multiple context categories simultaneously without creating duplicate copies.

2. Wardrobe Accessories vs. Complete Outfits Traditional Folders: Storing files in strict folders is like buying a pre-styled mannequin from a store window—the jacket, shirt, and pants are locked together as one fixed outfit.

Tagging: Tagging treats each document like an individual clothing item hanging in a modular closet. A pair of black boots can be tagged #Formal, #Winter, and #Leather.

The Art: Depending on what occasion you are dressing for (or what research question you are answering), you pull items based on the specific combination of tags you need at that exact moment.

3. The Constellation Map (Connecting the Dots) Traditional Folders: Think of a traditional filing cabinet as a row of isolated cardboard boxes stacked on a shelf. The contents of Box A cannot see or interact with Box B.

Tagging: Think of your document collection as a night sky full of stars. Each document is a single star. When you apply a tag like #Phase1 or #Theme, you light up a string that connects all stars bearing that tag, drawing a clear constellation across your entire universe of information.

The Art: Over time, overlapping tags reveal hidden patterns, clusters, and relationships between documents that would have otherwise remained buried inside separate folders.

Core Principles of Great Tagging Keep them broad yet specific: Use tags for cross-cutting attributes (e.g., #Status/Draft, #Source/Interview, #Topic/Equity).

Be consistent: Decide on a naming rule early on (e.g., lowercase vs. TitleCase) to avoid duplicate tags like #Research and #research.

Use them as dynamic filters: Combine two or three tags (e.g., #Phase1 + #HighPriority) to instantly isolate the exact subset of documents you need.

thematic viewTHEMATIC — the canvas re-composed and re-visualized around the ideas its documents share: terms that travel together across files are distilled into theme bodies, one per theme, sized by thematic weight and webbed by shared members. Click a theme to see its grounded member documents, click a member document to open it; toggling Thematic off restores the Main view untouched.

The Analogy: Imagine looking up at the night sky and grouping individual stars into constellations.

How It Works: Instead of looking at single files (stars), the canvas zooms out to highlight the overarching ideas (constellations) hidden across your text—such as Market Research, Budgeting, or Compliance. Each theme body is sized by how much content supports it, with glowing threads connecting themes that share common files. Clicking a constellation reveals the specific document stars within it; turning the view off snaps you right back to your individual file layout. Right-click a theme orb to show its associated documents as paper icons ringed around it; left-click an icon while that theme is the selected sun to reveal its row centered in the reader inventory.
ungrounded — a file no theme grounds: it carries none of the distilled ideas strongly enough to be a member. Each one still rides on exactly one theme orb — the theme whose terms hit its text hardest, or the largest theme when nothing hits — listed in that theme's UNGROUNDED section below the grounded members. Same rows, same clicks: clicking one drills down into the document.

Three ways to see the same canvas — Main shows every document body, Thematic rebuilds it from distilled text themes, Tags rebuilds it from shared tags. verse — short for universe: one project canvas — a grouping of documents with its own formation, sun, and lens. The whole-Visi|Docsverse thematic map is the one view that spans every verse at once.

Click a square to open the floating note editor; the reader and the Notes book tab show the same shared notepad (see "notes").
